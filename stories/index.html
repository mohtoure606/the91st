Gilles Aurelien

Midfielder · CDM / CM / CAM · Bracknell, England

“I know it's not gonna be easy. But I'm not gonna stop here.”

Where it started

Gilles Aurelien grew up in Bracknell, England, with a football at his feet and a father who believed in him before anyone else did. At seven, a scout invited him to trial at Southampton FC. He spent eight-plus years in the academy, traveling across Europe and winning Player of the Tournament in Spain.

The setback

COVID wiped out his post-academy trials. He and his dad chose America. He started at Otero Junior College, helped the program reach nationals for the first time in 12 years, then tore his ACL nine games into his sophomore season. His father flew from the UK for the surgery.

College

He came back. He transferred to Utah Valley University, became one of the best midfielders in the WAC, and graduated with a BS degree.

What comes next

After graduating, he returned to pursue professional opportunities. He was listed for the MLS SuperDraft. He has played against pros. He is not done.

Elijah Tanger

Forward / Winger · Nigeria / Canada

“Your ceiling is someone else's floor. Keep climbing.”

Where it started

Elijah Tanger was born in Nigeria, where football is not a hobby, it is life. From four years old, his father was his first coach. At twelve, his family immigrated to Canada.

The academy years

He earned a spot at Toronto FC Academy at U14, continued at the De Rosario Academy from U16 to U18, and trained at Phoenix Football Academy in Abuja.

The setback

COVID nearly closed the window. When it reopened, he earned a spot among the top sixty players across Canada.

Breaking through

In 2024 he trained with Real Monarchs of MLS Next Pro, won Player of the Year in the UPSL Mountain Division, and made the USL Academy All-Tournament Team. He was top scorer again in 2025.

What comes next

He is the first person in his family to go to college, and he is still chasing a professional contract.

Sharif Sayawu

Forward · #29 · Agona Duakwa, Ghana

“I left everything I knew. But I never left my dream.”

Where it started

Sharif Sayawu grew up in Agona Duakwa, a small town in Ghana where football is not something you choose, it is something that chooses you. His parents, Iddrisu Sayawu and Ayishatu Mohammed, believed in what they saw and sacrificed to make sure he had the chance to pursue it.

The academy

He earned his way to Phoenix Academy, one of Ghana's most respected development programs, which has sent players to MLS, USL, and NCAA Division I. He repaid their belief with 15 goals in 2023 and Best Player honors at the 2024 Showcase. The calls from America came. He chose Utah Valley University.

College

Getting on that plane was one of the hardest things he has ever done. He left his family, his country, and everything familiar, and arrived in Utah as a freshman. In his very first collegiate game he recorded two assists. Weeks later he scored his first Division I goal.

What comes next

Now Sharif is navigating a transfer, searching for the right program, and carrying the hopes of a family back in Ghana. His story is just beginning.
