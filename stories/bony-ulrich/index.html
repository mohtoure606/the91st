His story.

Quick, confident winger developed at Right to Dream Academy, with European club experience. Now a junior at Lindsey Wilson University.

Bony Ulrich grew up in Abidjan, Côte d'Ivoire. His mother wanted him in school, so he snuck out to play. Some mornings he woke up at 4:30 and walked an hour to practice with the other boys, waking each other up and hopping trucks to get there.

Read his full story

Scouted at nine

At nine, he showed up to a trial with dyed hair so he would stand out among more than a hundred kids. It worked. He was picked for Right to Dream, the academy in Ghana, and left home to chase the game. In his words, the hardest part of moving to Ghana was the food.

Europe, and the clubs that said no

Right to Dream took him to FC Nordsjælland in Denmark and to tournaments and trials across Europe. Over the years he has played in more than 25 countries. Teammates he started with went on to play professionally at the top level, while trial after trial ended with a version of the same answer: they wanted bigger players. A bad experience with an agent cost him a contract.

Almost walking away

During COVID, caught between Spain and Norway and told to go back to Ghana, he came close to quitting. Along the way, a mentor, a Ghanaian professional player who later passed away on the field, talked him out of giving up on the game.

America

He rebuilt his career in the United States at Salt Lake Community College and moved on to Lindsey Wilson University, where he is a junior in 2026. Off the pitch he describes himself as shy. On it, he says, he is different. His long-term goal is to become an agent himself, so young players get the guidance he didn't.

“Outside the field, I'm a shy person though. But on the field, nah, I'm different.”
